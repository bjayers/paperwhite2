# Ecological encounters: rules questionnaire

The first step of the encounter redesign (see `README.md` in this folder). Each question has a **proposed default**;
answer by confirming it, changing it, or writing your own. Add anything the questions miss under **Your notes** at
the end of each section, or in chat. Your answers become `rules.md`, which the generator follows.

Already decided (2026-10-31):
- Areas stay as they are (the 135 original tables), and the 7 methods are enough.
- Every family is obtainable somewhere. Legendaries and mythicals are never wild: they get events. Some special
  Pokémon (Zorua, Larvesta, the full list to come) are static encounters, gifts or trades. Some species are only had
  by evolving or breeding, and trade evolutions will get evolution levels.
- Season-, time- and weather-only Pokémon are encouraged where they make sense.
- A clean slate: the ported tables are only a source of ideas.

What the game can express, for reference: 7 methods (grass, dark grass, rustling, surf, rippling surf, fishing,
rippling fishing); 4 seasons; 5 periods (morning, day, evening, night, late night); weather (sunny, rain, gusty rain,
thunderstorm, snow, blizzard, diamond dust, sandstorm, and the others of the weather calendar); 5 rarity tiers
(common 55%, uncommon 25%, rare 12%, very rare 7%, exotic 1% of a method's encounters, each split among the Pokémon
in it); story conditions (flags and variables); single, double and triple battles; daily outbreaks; levels that
scale with the party, within each species' minimum and maximum.

## A. Philosophy

**A1. What matters most, in order?** Ecological realism; variety when you return to a place; a good choice of team
members along the story; challenge.
- Proposed: realism first, then variety, then team choice; challenge comes from the levels and the trainers rather
  than from what's found.
- Your answer: Ecological realism is a priority, but we should also consider variety and team choice.  It's not a
  difficulty hack, but it's alright to have some "organic" difficulty that arises from Pokemon placements.

**A2. Which sources count as "canon" for where a Pokémon lives?** Real animals and plants it's based on; Pokédex
entries (every game); the anime; where it lives in other regions' games.
- Proposed: Pokédex entries and real ecology carry the most weight, other regions' placements next, the anime as a
  tiebreaker. Made-up ecology is fine where it fits the Pokédex and the real animal.
- Your answer: In-game fantasy (from every game) is the priority—anything that is mentioned in Pokedex entries, 
  dialogue, etc.  Then other official sources after (anime, manga, TCG cards, and other game regions).  I will also
  provide some noteworthy/relevant data from the official Pokecology book (some encounter placements are alreadyderived from this book).

**A3. How "Unova" should it feel?** Unova is based on the New York area; its own Pokémon (Gen 5) could be the
backbone, with others filling niches.
- Proposed: Gen 5 species form the backbone of most areas (roughly half or more of a route's Pokémon); other
  generations fill niches Gen 5 lacks, and each has a reason to be there (see B).
- Your answer: Unovan Pokemon should feel predominant most of the time, but not so strictly that others' placments
  become unrealistic or too hard to find.

Your notes: Ecology is actually the core reason I started making this hack.  It just evolved quite a bit when I
  what was possible.  I wanted to make a Pokemon game where every Pokemon was available, but not nonsensically crammed into every free slot like many fan-made hacks do.  I also liked the idea of having some Pokemon only available in certain seasons—like birds that migrate semi-realistically, bears that hibernate, etc.—instead of again cramming them all into a single non-seasonal encounter table (which I've seen several BW/BW2 hacks do).  It would create a more immersive and atmospheric world out of my favorite region in Pokemon, where the experience might differ based on what season you begin playing in. Another core idea is that placements tell a story.  I like to encounter Pokemon in an area and ask what its placement is telling me about the species or the world.  Or find that a Pokemon is less common in another season and ask "why?"

## B. Native, migrant and introduced Pokémon

**B1. How do non-Unova Pokémon get here?** Possible reasons: native all along (just not in the original games);
migrants passing through in some seasons; introduced (escaped pets, cargo at Castelia's docks, Pokémon
released by trainers); relics (ancient Pokémon in ruins and deep caves).
- Proposed: every non-Gen-5 family gets one of these reasons in its dictionary entry, which decides its places and
  rarity: natives like any other; migrants only in their seasons; introduced ones near people (cities, docks,
  parks, dumps) and mostly uncommon to very rare; relics rare, in ruins and old caves.
- Your answer: For most, we'll simply assume it was native all along or travels there in migration.  This is probably the least 
  "realistic" concession we'll have to make to have all Pokemon available in one game.  However, I like your idea that
  some may be introduced around people.  I've already done this to a degree without thinking, with other regional cats
  and various domestic animals available in Castelia.  But if it makes sense for it to reside elsewhere in the world too,
  it's ok to do so.  "Ancient" Pokemon and relics like Unown will probably still be available in relevant locations.  Some fossils
  might even be available too, rarely (where made-up data applies, like it sometimes does in Pokecology).

**B2. What is the Exotic tier (1%) for?**
- Proposed: vagrants and oddities, a Pokémon well outside its usual range (a lost seabird inland, an escaped pet). At
  most one or two per area, and never a family's only source.
- Your answer: I'm considering using this more as a gameplay consideration than a true "exotic" slot.  While I still want the placements to make sense, I've always enjoyed searching to certain rare or sought-after Pokemon in iconic places.  For  example, I'll always have fond memories of searching for that Ralts near Petalburg on nearly every playthrough of  Emerald back in the day.  The plan is to make these more common from Rustling grass (maybe 10%), but still rarely available from normal grass.  I'm not opposed to using it as a stray or lost Pokemon on some routes where there is a good explanation for it. (Like overhunting,  Pokemon that are very rare in one season, common in another, and non-existant in others, etc.)

Your notes: A big inspiration for this hack for me was migrating bird Pokemon, so I want to put a particular focus on these, 
  and maybe use it as inspiration for other species.  I thought it would be very cool to see some birds make their home in warm
  parts of the region in certain seasons, colder parts in summer, etc., while some only visit in certain seasons and return home 
  for others.  Maybe some residents don't leave the region but inhabit different parts of the region in different seasons, following
  their prey, which may become rarer when their predators are around (like Swellow and Yanma).  And maybe some, like Pidove or Murkrow, simply
  stick around human settlements year round, with their behavior changing at different seasons, times of day, weathers, etc.

## C. Progression and availability

**C1. Should the early game offer a balanced choice of types?**
- Proposed: yes, loosely. By the third gym, each type should be catchable somewhere (perhaps rare), without
  forcing unnatural placements; where nature gives a gap, a gift or trade can fill it.
- Your answer: Loosely.  Again, I do like the idea of options changing based on the season, to a degree.  For example,
  That Riolu in Floccesy Ranch that many like to catch to counter the first gym might not be around in every season.  In the current
  encounter tables, I have it there only in Summer and Autumn.  It moves to Virbank Complex 2 in Winter and Spring, which isn't
  accessible until *after* the first gym.  Little changes like this mix things up, while telling a story about the Pokemon too.

**C2. Should story progress unlock Pokémon?** Story conditions can gate a habitat behind a badge or an event.
- Proposed: sparingly: for strong Pokémon on early routes (a powerful predator that "moves in" later), and for the
  post-game. Not for ordinary species.
- Your answer: I'm considering having little side missions to unlock some wild encounters, but I should probably add those manually as
  events.  I like the idea of some powerful Pokemon moving in later on, though.  It could be a way to place a Pokemon in an ecologically
  fitting early area without it throwing off the balance too much.  I'd want a good explanation as to *why* this would happen, of course,
  just like for most other Pokemon placements, but I'm open to the idea.  Another goal of this is to make it more desireable for the
  player to return to old routes (hence the scaling levels), so this could further serve that goal.

**C3. Where do the strongest families go?** Pseudo-legendaries (Dratini, Larvitar, Bagon, Beldum, Gible, Deino) and
other powerful lines (Riolu, Axew, the fossils' living relatives...).
- Proposed: late or remote areas, rare or very rare, often restricted by season, time or weather; their base stage
  only, never an evolved form, unless you'd like late-game exceptions.
- Your answer: Mostly late game, but prioritize realistic placements.  If necessary, use the badge-gating idea.

**C4. Starters of every generation?**
- Proposed: not wild; gifts or events (list them under the exceptions).
- Your answer: I'm torn on starters.  On one hand, I want them to exist ecologically but rarely; but they're also quite special.
  I've settled for putting most of them in hidden grottoes.  This isn't ideal, but I think it fits a sweet spot, for the most part.
  A caveat is that the Unovan starters are only found in grottoes only available after beating the game, so the starter choice is still
  important.
  Furthermore, two starters in particular are available in the wild due to mentions in Pokecology that I liked: Piplup near Iccirus City 
  in Winter (because Glalie hunts Piplup, Glalie also comes down from Twist Mountain to hunt it), and Charmander near Reversal Mountain.
  The story/ecological reason trumps the specialness of the starter, but we'll still consider them special and placed in meaningful places.

**C5. Baby Pokémon (Pichu, Riolu, Munchlax...)?**
- Proposed: by breeding only, except where a baby is the family's natural wild form (Riolu, perhaps Happiny in
  safe, people-heavy places); decided per family.
- Your answer: Many will be available in the wild with a lower max level.  Only a few will be breeding-only.  I'm trying to think about
  what families of Pokemon would exist together in the wild.  Some might travel in packs but still have babies around, for instance.  They 
  may be rarer than the adults, though.

**C6. Fossil Pokémon?**
- Proposed: by fossils only, never wild.
- Your answer: Almost exclusively by fossils only.  A select few mentioned in Pokecology in a meaningful way may be available very rarely
  in the wild.

**C7. The post-game and the late routes (Routes 21–23, Abundant Shrine, Giant Chasm...)?**
- Proposed: home to the rarest families and the evolved forms of strong lines, and to "national" species that make
  completing the Pokédex a post-game activity.
- Your answer: Still inspired by ecology, but many of those areas still have potential for rare species, like the Dreamyard (for psychics) or Giant Chasm
(for those space-adjacent Pokemon)

Your notes: In short, I do want some Pokemon to feel special and/or be harder to find, but I also want those placements to be meaningful
  and based on something.  It should tell a story *and* feel special.

## D. Evolution stages in the wild

**D1. Should evolved forms appear in the wild?**
- Proposed: yes, as in nature (adults and young together), but:
  - an evolved form is always rarer than its base form in the same place;
  - evolved forms appear only where the area's level band reaches about their evolution level (the scaled level
    keeps them from appearing too early);
  - final stages of three-stage families mostly in dark grass, rippling water and late areas.
- Your answer: Level bands for individual areas are actually mostly irrelevant now because of the scaling and minimum levels at which the adults
  can appear.  This way we can have those older species become available realistically alongside younger species without compromising gameplay.
  For evolutions, many will be available in the wild, but not all.  I'm unsure how best to determine which are and which aren't as of now.

**D2. Forms that evolve by stone, friendship, trade or other methods (Vileplume, Crobat, Gengar...)?**
- Proposed: wild only where the lore supports it (a Moon Stone site, a haunted place), and rare; otherwise by
  evolution only.
- Your answer: I agree with that.  For example, if it evolves around a leaf stone or moon-related site, it's reasonable that a Pokemon 
  that could evolve from it could evolve when exposed to it in the environment.  Chargestone Cave is another example (maybe a good spot for Raichu?).
  Friendship evolutions probably wouldn't be in the wild at all, unless there's a good reason for it.  Trade evolutions will be made level evolutions eventually, so we’ll make them available but rare.

**D3. Families that branch (Eevee, Tyrogue, Wurmple, Snorunt...)?**
- Proposed: the base form wild; branches by evolution only, unless a branch's ecology clearly differs (Glalie in
  icy caves, Froslass in haunted snowy places, as rare).
- Your answer: Branches could exist in places where there are clearly "counterpart" areas.  I think I did something like this on Route 11, which has 
  an upper level with normal grass and a lower level with dark grass.

Your notes: Forms are actually an important thing to note as well; good catch.  Forms should be available where they make sense, like Deerling and Sawsbuck’s forms in various seasons.

## E. What an area holds

**E1. How many Pokémon per area and method?** (The game allows up to 64; the original tables had about 6–12 per
method.)
- Proposed targets, before seasons and times:
  - grass: 8–14 species over the year, about 5–8 at any one moment;
  - dark grass: the grass's species minus the weakest plus 2–4 of its own (more evolved, rarer);
  - surf: 3–6; rippling surf: 2–4, rarer and more evolved;
  - fishing: 4–8; rippling fishing: 2–4;
  - rustling: see E5.
- Your answer: This generally sounds good.  We’ll have loose limits for each area, but we’ll prioritize the Pokémon’s habitats and what makes the most sense for it.  In general, stronger BST species will prefer dark grass.  Dark grass is also no longer the “double battle” grass, exclusively, nor does it innately have higher level Pokémon in it.  Double and triple battles can spawn for specific species that might hunt in packs.  More on that later.  Water methods also no longer need to have a smaller number of spawns; we ditched the old tables to free us from the slots, after all; however, there are fewer water Pokémon, so this may balance itself out.

**E2. Type diversity?**
- Proposed: no quota; ecology decides. The generator only warns where an area has one type for most of its
  encounters, so you can decide.
- Your answer: No quota, and we’ll leave the warning if things look too generic in an area.

**E3. Food chains?**
- Proposed: yes: predators less common than their prey in the same place, and several predator-prey pairs per area
  (Sandile and Darumaka's prey in the desert, Pidove and the bugs). A herbivore or bug base, few apex predators.
- Your answer: This is exactly the kind of thing I want to consider.  We’ll consider it across seasons, areas, and to a lesser extent, times of day and weather.  Lapras overhunting fish in summer waters, Swellow hunting Yanma, etc.  Some prey may come out more (or exclusively) at night because predators are asleep and vice versa.  Same for weathers.

**E4. Widespread versus local species?**
- Proposed: a few generalists widespread and common (Patrat, Pidove, Purrloin, Lillipup and their counterparts);
  most species in a few neighbouring areas of their habitat; specialists in one or two places. Neighbouring areas of
  one biome share some species, so the land changes gradually.
- Your answer: I agree.  We’re already doing that to a degree, and we’ll factor it in as well.  We’ll just be careful not to make them *too* ubiquitous.

**E5. Rustling spots: shaking grass, dust clouds and bridge shadows?** They already give Audino (30%) and items (30%),
and otherwise the area's Rare, Very rare and Exotic Pokémon.
- Proposed: give them Pokémon of their own where they make sense: burrowing Pokémon in dust clouds (Drilbur, the
  Diglett and Sandshrew families); birds in bridge shadows (Ducklett, Wingull, Pelipper, Swanna); shy or hiding
  Pokémon in shaking grass.
- Your answer: I agree with this.  I was planning to also make them more reliable spots to find the “exotic” rarity Pokémon in addition to the specialty ones that fit.

**E6. What is dark grass, ecologically?**
- Proposed: deeper, older vegetation: larger, more evolved and more nocturnal Pokémon, and the place for doubles.
- Your answer: It’s no longer limited to doubles; double and triple wild battles can now happen anywhere except fishing, and we’ll reserve those for Pokémon that hunt in packs, call for help, or otherwise appear together.  Ecologically… I’m not sure I want to stack it with more conditional Pokémon, like those that are nocturnal.  I feel like it should be more of a gameplay consideration to keep the normal grass tables from getting too crowded, and a place to put stronger (higher BST) species.  But let’s also try not to split families between normal and dark grass; either put them in only one type or in both.

**E7. Rippling water?**
- Proposed: a disturbance from something big: the evolved and rarer water Pokémon, and the bigger fish.
- Your answer: Exactly that.  It’ll also double with the same purpose as rustling grass, shadows, or dust clouds, but the general idea is a disturbance by a bigger fish.  For surfing, maybe a school of fish, if the species fits it (double or triple battle)

Your notes:  Basically, it’s a mix of ecology and gameplay considerations.  We’re no longer limited, and we wanted to be free of those limitations, but they were also there in the first place for game design reasons, so it’s worth considering them to some degree—or rewriting them, like with dark grass now being just an alternative habitat to regular grass with stronger and/or different species.  Maybe ecologically we should consider it “taller, thicker grass,” in a way.  It’s the ”wilder” grass that’s rougher, tougher, and less maintained (in addition to the gameplay considerations).

## F. Seasons, times and weather

**F1. How strict should time of day be?**
- Proposed: three strengths, chosen per family:
  - *strict*: only in its periods (strongly nocturnal or diurnal Pokémon: Hoothoot, Murkrow at night);
  - *preferred*: one tier more common in its periods, one tier rarer outside them;
  - *none*: the same all day.
  Most families "preferred"; "strict" for a memorable few.
- Your answer: That’s exactly what I was thinking.  We’re also tentatively splitting the times into only “Day” (consisting of morning, day, and evening) and “Night” (consisting of night and late night).  All 5 options are available if a species really would *only* show up in the morning or *only* very late at night, but we’ll use that conservatively.  Only Pokémon that would truly be nocturnal or diurnal *only* appear at certain times (Hoothoot, Sunflora, maybe).  Others might be rarer or more common depending on the time but always available.  The rest are always available.

**F2. Seasons?**
- Proposed: seasonal behaviour from each family's ecology:
  - migration: migrants arrive and leave with the seasons (many birds south for winter, some arriving for it);
  - hibernation: bears, many bugs and reptiles scarce or absent in winter;
  - blooming: grass and bug Pokémon at their most common in spring and summer;
  - winter: ice Pokémon spread to places they can't live in otherwise;
  - seasonal forms (Deerling, Sawsbuck) follow the season as now.
- Your answer: Spot on with these.  Season is a big one because it’s Unova’s “gimmick,” so we’ll almost always consider it.  Same as with time, some Pokémon will only ever be available in one or two seasons, others will be available more often but maybe with different rarities in each, and others will be year-round.  Ice Pokémon may be more common in areas that get colder in winter, like Swinub in Routes 19 and 20.

**F3. Weather?**
- Proposed: weather shifts rarity by about one tier: rain brings water, bug and grass Pokémon; thunderstorms
  electric ones; snow and blizzards ice; sandstorms ground and rock. A few Pokémon are found only in a weather
  (for example, a thunderstorm-only electric Pokémon on an open route).
- Your answer:  I agree with this too.  There are some weathers, like Thunderstorms, that I’m hoping to make more common on some routes.  I’m open to your suggestions on which routes to do this on based on where you place some Pokémon.

**F4. Reachability.** Your season setting can fix one season, and players may not play late at night.
- Proposed: every family can be found by an ordinary player: at least one place where it appears in morning, day or
  evening, or in at least two seasons, or a breeding/evolution route. Strict late-night or one-season Pokémon need a
  second way to get them. Or is it fine that some need a fixed season or a late-night session?
- Your answer: My intention is to make some species harder to get by only being available under certain conditions.  All Pokémon are obtainable, but it’ll require a longer term play period, some luck, and playing the game at different times of day.  Additionally, players may have different experiences by playing or replaying at different times.  I want that sense of discovery and exploration to exist strongly.

## G. Rarity

**G1. What do the tiers mean?**
- Proposed: *common*: the place's dominant populations. *Uncommon*: regulars. *Rare*: there, but seldom seen
  (predators, shy species). *Very rare*: a few individuals (strong lines, specialists at the edge of their range).
  *Exotic*: vagrants and oddities (B2).
- Your answer: This is more of a replacement for the hard-coded encounter slots from before, implemented in a way that can scale if there are too many or too few Pokémon in an area (so an area with 1 common Pokémon and 1 exotic one would scale to 98% common and 2% exotic).  The name exotic is mostly just because I didn’t know what to place above “very rare,” it doesn’t necessarily mean it’s an “exotic” Pokémon in the area.  We’ll use it as described above—for trophy Pokemon, which will be a bit more common from rustling grass.

**G2. Should a family's rarity follow its strength?** (Base stat total, or evolution stage.)
- Proposed: partly. Strength sets a ceiling (a pseudo-legendary is never common), ecology sets the rest (a weak
  Pokémon can be rare if it's shy or specialised).
- Your answer: Yes, to a degree, but not completely.  So your take is a fair one.

Your notes: Properly defining rarity is something I struggled with on the last iteration of “ecological encounters” as well.  In the end, I think it should be what makes sense to be rarer (overhunter species, shy species, camouflaged species, like Kekleon, etc.) and “trophy” Pokémon that are commonly sought-after.

## H. Doubles, triples and outbreaks

**H1. Which Pokémon come in doubles or triples?** (Each species has single, double and triple chances.)
- Proposed: social Pokémon only: herds (Deerling, Bouffalant, Mareep), flocks (Pidove, Ducklett), schools
  (Basculin), packs (Lillipup, Poochyena), colonies (Combee, Durant). Triples rare. Solitary and territorial Pokémon
  always alone.
- Your answer: This was my thought as well.  The more social a species is, the higher a chance it will be a triple battle, otherwise a double.  Solitary and territorial will be always alone.  Most species will probably have double battles possible, but it will be a very rare chance, since all grass allows it now.  Triple is reserved for highly social species.  There is one other wrinkle, though: we added an option to restrict double and triple battles only to that family of Pokémon that trigger it (only Poochyena and Mightyena would appear together, for instance) or allow any Pokémon from the area to join in.  I have a feeling it’ll mostly be the former, but they might be places for any Pokémon as well to make wild battles more interesting.

**H2. Partners: the same family, or anything in the area?**
- Proposed: the same family (a herd with young and adults), which is the default now.
- Your answer:  Same family is usually in an area.  And you don’t have to limit early stages to early routes and later stages to later routes anymore because of the scaling and min level values.  For instance, it’s ok to put Pidove’s entire line on Route 19 because only Pidove would be available at the start, and the others would gradually become available as the player levels up and progresses.

**H3. What are outbreaks for?** One area each day after `FLAG_SWARMS`, taking 40% of its encounters.
- Proposed: two uses: a rare Pokémon of the area made common for a day (a hatching, a bloom); and migrants or
  wanderers in a place that suits them but isn't their home. Some families' best source could be an outbreak.
- Your answer: I’m actually still undecided on outbreaks, and they may change later.  The conditional encounters (time, weather) work similarly to how outbreaks work.  Tentatively, I like your idea of rare Pokémon in the area being more common for the day.  As of now, though, I don’t think we can specify a season or other conditions for it, though. so that may be a good idea for improvement.

## I. Levels

**I1. Levels scale with the party (80% of its median, ±3), within each species' minimum and maximum.**
- Proposed: a species' minimum is its evolution level minus 2 (as the import used) for evolved forms, and none for
  base forms; its maximum stays open, except where you want a base form to stop appearing at high levels (a
  Caterpie in the late game?).
- Your answer: The level formula isn’t set in stone yet, and still needs improvement, but it’s meant to allow more realistic encounters without as much need to account for when in the game the area is unlocked.  Pinwheel Forest is late-game, but it does make sense for Caterpie and other bugs to be there, after all—there’s just also a chance for very high level Beedrill or Scolipede, for example.  Something like this was already done with fishing in vanilla, where fish could often be an 15-50 or so at random (it just wasn’t scaled quite the way ours is).

Your notes: Basically, don’t worry too much about the *placement* of Pokémon due to levels; just make sure it makes sense ecologically.  For the actual level ranges, we’ll mostly keep them roughly (not exactly) to the level the species actually evolves (including for those that evolve under special conditions with no exact number; we’ll still guess at it).
