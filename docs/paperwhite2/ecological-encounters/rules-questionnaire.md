# Ecological encounters: rules questionnaire

The first step of the encounter redesign (see `README.md` in this folder). Each question has a **proposed default**;
answer by confirming it, changing it, or writing your own. Add anything the questions miss under **Your notes** at
the end of each section, or in chat. Your answers become `rules.md`, which the generator follows.

Already decided (2026-10-31):
- Areas stay as they are (the 135 original tables), and the 7 methods are enough.
- Every family is obtainable somewhere. Legendaries and mythicals are never wild: they get events. Some special
  Pokémon (Zorua, Larvesta, the full list to come) are static encounters, gifts or trades. Some species are only had
  by evolving or breeding, and trade evolutions will get evolution levels.
- Season-, time- and weather-only Pokémon are encouraged where they make sense.
- A clean slate: the ported tables are only a source of ideas.

What the game can express, for reference: 7 methods (grass, dark grass, rustling, surf, rippling surf, fishing,
rippling fishing); 4 seasons; 5 periods (morning, day, evening, night, late night); weather (sunny, rain, gusty rain,
thunderstorm, snow, blizzard, diamond dust, sandstorm, and the others of the weather calendar); 5 rarity tiers
(common 55%, uncommon 25%, rare 12%, very rare 7%, exotic 1% of a method's encounters, each split among the Pokémon
in it); story conditions (flags and variables); single, double and triple battles; daily outbreaks; levels that
scale with the party, within each species' minimum and maximum.

## B. Native, migrant and introduced Pokémon

**B1. How do non-Unova Pokémon get here?** Possible reasons: native all along (just not in the original games);
migrants passing through in some seasons; introduced (escaped pets, cargo at Castelia's docks, Pokémon
released by trainers); relics (ancient Pokémon in ruins and deep caves).
- Proposed: every non-Gen-5 family gets one of these reasons in its dictionary entry, which decides its places and
  rarity: natives like any other; migrants only in their seasons; introduced ones near people (cities, docks,
  parks, dumps) and mostly uncommon to very rare; relics rare, in ruins and old caves.

## F. Seasons, times and weather

**F1. How strict should time of day be?**
- Proposed: three strengths, chosen per family:
  - *strict*: only in its periods (strongly nocturnal or diurnal Pokémon: Hoothoot, Murkrow at night);
  - *preferred*: one tier more common in its periods, one tier rarer outside them;
  - *none*: the same all day.
  Most families "preferred"; "strict" for a memorable few.
- Your answer:

**F2. Seasons?**
- Proposed: seasonal behaviour from each family's ecology:
  - migration: migrants arrive and leave with the seasons (many birds south for winter, some arriving for it);
  - hibernation: bears, many bugs and reptiles scarce or absent in winter;
  - blooming: grass and bug Pokémon at their most common in spring and summer;
  - winter: ice Pokémon spread to places they can't live in otherwise;
  - seasonal forms (Deerling, Sawsbuck) follow the season as now.
- Your answer:

**F3. Weather?**
- Proposed: weather shifts rarity by about one tier: rain brings water, bug and grass Pokémon; thunderstorms
  electric ones; snow and blizzards ice; sandstorms ground and rock. A few Pokémon are found only in a weather
  (for example, a thunderstorm-only electric Pokémon on an open route).
- Your answer:

**F4. Reachability.** Your season setting can fix one season, and players may not play late at night.
- Proposed: every family can be found by an ordinary player: at least one place where it appears in morning, day or
  evening, or in at least two seasons, or a breeding/evolution route. Strict late-night or one-season Pokémon need a
  second way to get them. Or is it fine that some need a fixed season or a late-night session?

## G. Rarity

**G1. What do the tiers mean?**
- Proposed: *common*: the place's dominant populations. *Uncommon*: regulars. *Rare*: there, but seldom seen
  (predators, shy species). *Very rare*: a few individuals (strong lines, specialists at the edge of their range).
  *Exotic*: vagrants and oddities (B2).
