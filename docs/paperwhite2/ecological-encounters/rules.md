# Ecological encounters: the rules

The placement rules the dictionaries and the generator follow, from the user's answers (`rules-questionnaire.md`,
2026-11-10) and the system's facts (`system.md`). Rules can change as we go; a change goes here with its date.

## 1. The aim

- **Placements tell a story.** A player who meets a Pokémon somewhere, or misses it in another season, should be able
  to ask "why?" and find an answer in the world: its habitat, its food, its predators, the season, the weather, the
  people nearby. Every placement has a reason (kept in the review reports, not written in the game).
- **Ecology first, with variety and team choice close behind.** Not a difficulty hack; difficulty that arises from
  placements is fine.
- **Every family is obtainable somewhere**, but not crammed in: some take patience, luck, a season, a time of day or a
  weather. Players who start in different seasons, or replay, should have different experiences. Discovery matters.
- **Unovan Pokémon predominate**, but not so strictly that other placements become unrealistic or too hard to find.

## 2. Sources, in order

1. In-game fiction of every game: Pokédex entries, NPC dialogue, location descriptions.
2. Other official sources: the anime, the manga, the TCG, other regions' games.
3. The Pokécology notes (in `rules-questionnaire.md`, section J), some of which the user already used.
4. Real ecology of the animal or plant a Pokémon is based on.
5. Made-up ecology, where it fits all of the above.

## 3. Where non-Unovan Pokémon come from

- Most are simply **native** (never seen in the original games) or **migrants**.
- Some are **introduced** around people: domestic animals and other regions' cats in Castelia, and similar, where it
  fits; they can live elsewhere too if that makes sense.
- **Ancient** Pokémon and relics (Unown and the like) live in fitting ruins and old places.

## 3a. Places with conditions of their own

- **The Nature Preserve** is unlocked by completing Pokédex numbers 494 to 649 (2026-11-13). No Pokémon of Unova
  (494-649) may be found only there; only Pokémon of generations 1 to 4 may be exclusive to it.
- **Reversal Mountain's two cave sets** are switched at any Pokémon Center, any number of times; new saves start as
  White 2 (the Fiery Caves).

## 4. Who is never wild

- Legendaries and mythicals (events).
- Special Pokémon given as static encounters, gifts or trades (Zorua, Larvesta, the list to come; Zorua perhaps as a
  "child" NPC in Nimbasa who runs away, or rarely battles).
- Most starters: the user places them in Hidden Grottoes by hand (the Unovan ones only after the game), except where a
  story places one in the wild (Piplup near Icirrus in winter with Glalie hunting it; Charmander rarely outside
  Reversal Mountain).
- Fossils, almost always (a few from Pokécology, very rarely).
- Trade evolutions, mostly (a Link Cable item will evolve them).
- Friendship evolutions, unless there's a good reason.
- Some species are had only by evolution or breeding; a few babies only by breeding.

Hidden Grottoes are set by hand; the generator counts them as a source but never writes them.

## 5. Evolution stages

- Decided per family in its dictionary entry, with a reason. Defaults: level evolutions are wild where adults and
  young plausibly share a place; stone evolutions only where the environment has that stone's influence (leaf stone
  or moon sites; Raichu in Chargestone Cave); friendship and trade evolutions not wild.
- Branches (Eevee, Tyrogue, Wurmple, Snorunt...) can be wild where an area has clear counterpart places (Route 11's
  upper grass and lower dark grass).
- **Later stages live wherever their family does** (2026-11-14): with level scaling, an early route still has its
  evolved forms at high levels (a level 20 Pidove capped at its maximum, beside a level 75 Unfezant). A home limits
  itself to some stages only where a story says so (Swanna only in winter, Gyarados only in vast waters, a trophy's
  base form).
- **Powerful Pokémon met early** (2026-11-14) can have a raised minimum level (about 40) to keep them off the tables
  until later (Gible in Relic Passage); most first stages still start at about 1. Only places before Driftveil need
  this (the Southwest, Castelia and its sewers, Route 4, the desert, Relic Passage, Nimbasa and Route 5): later
  places are past the fifth badge, where wild levels are over 33 anyway. The Southeast (Routes 1 to 3, Striaton, the Dreamyard,
  Pinwheel Forest) is post-game in B2W2, so anything there is safe. Skarmory stays low at Virbank Complex: the
  user placed it there as a counter to the second gym.
- **Older stages deeper** (2026-11-14): a family can show its young near a cave's surface and its older stages only
  deeper in (Gible in Relic Passage, Gabite in Relic Castle's deepest rooms, Garchomp in Victory Road's caves), so
  it stays one family across places with some flair in where the old ones live.
- **Levels don't decide placement.** A whole line can live on one route: only the base form appears at first, and the
  others as the party's level reaches their minimums. Placements follow ecology; levels follow the party.
- **Level ranges:** a basic form from about level 1 to its evolution level + about 3; an evolved form from about its
  evolution level (guessed for stones, friendship and other conditions). Not exact.
- Babies are often wild, rarer than the adults, with a lower maximum level; families can appear together (packs with
  their young).
- Forms appear where they make sense (Deerling and Sawsbuck by season, with the form set in the data).

## 6. What an area holds

- **Loose targets** per area and method, before seasons and times: grass 8–14 species over the year (5–8 at one
  moment); dark grass a similar number; surf and fishing as many as fit (fewer water Pokémon exist, so it balances
  itself). The Pokémon's habitats come first; the targets only warn. Counts are of families, not species, since a family shows its stages by the level gate (2026-11-14). A crowded table is fine now and then where it's believable (a forest at
  the height of summer), as long as it doesn't go overboard and the commons don't become rare (2026-11-14).
- **Grass and dark grass** are usually split for gameplay, to keep each list from getting too long; they're the same
  ground side by side (Route 5). Where they're separate places that differ (Route 1's meadow and its pine hills
  across the water), they're different habitats. Each place's entry says which (2026-11-13).
- **Dark grass** is the "wilder" grass: taller, thicker, rougher, less maintained. It holds stronger (higher base
  stat) and different species, and keeps the grass from getting crowded. It isn't for doubles, higher levels or
  nocturnal Pokémon as such. **A family isn't split between grass and dark grass in one area:** it's in one or both.
- **Rippling water** is a disturbance by something big: the evolved and rarer water Pokémon, bigger fish; in surf, a
  school (a double or triple) where the species fits. It doesn't fill itself, so it needs its own habitats.
- **Rustling spots** fill themselves with the area's Rare, Very rare and Exotic grass Pokémon (Audino keeps its 30% of
  shaking grass; items 30% of dust clouds and shadows). Give them species of their own only where a story calls for
  it: burrowers in dust clouds, birds in bridge shadows, hiding Pokémon in shaking grass.
- **Type diversity:** no quota; the generator warns where an area looks too uniform.
- **Food chains** across seasons and areas, less so times and weather: predators rarer than their prey; prey that come
  out at night when their predators sleep, or the reverse; predators that follow their prey (Swellow and Yanma);
  overhunting (Lapras abundant near Humilau in summer, fewer fish then).
- **Widespread versus local:** a few generalists widespread and common, never too ubiquitous; most species in a few
  neighbouring areas of their habitat; specialists in one or two places. Neighbouring areas of one biome share
  species, so the land changes gradually. With so many conditions, there's no need to be strict: most Pokémon can be
  anywhere they make sense, and only a few are limited to one place (2026-11-13). A trophy can be a trophy in several
  places (Feebas). A Pokémon suggested for one place isn't exclusive to it: it lives wherever it fits (at
  night, in winter...), unless it's meant to be very rare or to have one home (2026-11-13).

## 7. Seasons, times and weather

- **Season** is Unova's gimmick and almost always matters: some Pokémon only in one or two seasons, others all year
  with different rarities, others the same all year.
  - **Almost every exterior feels a bit different in each season while keeping its identity** (2026-11-14): a few
    families come and go, or change their rarity, with each season, round a core that stays all year.
  - Migration is a centrepiece, birds above all: some live in warm parts in one season and cold parts in another;
    some only visit; residents shift within the region following their prey; town birds (Pidove, Murkrow) stay all
    year but change with the season, time and weather.
  - Hibernation (bears, many bugs and reptiles scarce or gone in winter); blooming (grass and bugs most common in
    spring and summer); ice Pokémon spreading where it gets cold in winter (Swinub on Routes 19 and 20).
- **Time** is mostly Day (morning, day, evening) or Night (night, late night). Three strengths per family: *strict*
  (only in its periods: Hoothoot, perhaps Sunflora), *preferred* (more common in its periods, rarer outside), *none*.
  Single periods (only morning, only late night) sparingly.
- **Mist** (`WEATHER_TINT_9`) is never a condition: the Habitat Dex shows it as clear (2026-11-13).
- **Weather** shifts rarity by about a tier (rain: water, bug, grass; thunderstorms: electric; snow and blizzards:
  ice; sandstorms: ground and rock), and a few Pokémon appear only in a weather. Weather can be added to any area
  where it makes sense (the user will make it easy to edit); the area dictionary suggests where, conservatively, and
  where a weather would make a placement most interesting.
- **Reachability:** some families deliberately need long play, luck and playing at different times. Every family is
  still obtainable.

## 8. Rarity

- The tiers replace the old fixed slots and scale with what's there (`system.md`).
- What makes a Pokémon rarer: being a predator or an overhunter, shy, camouflaged (Kecleon), specialised, at the edge
  of its range, or strong (strength sets a ceiling: a pseudo-legendary is never common; ecology sets the rest).
- **Exotic is the trophy tier:** sought-after Pokémon in iconic places (the Ralts near Petalburg), very rare in grass and
  more common in the area's rustling spots (which draw on it automatically). It can also hold a stray with a good
  explanation.
- The strongest families (pseudo-legendaries and other powerful lines) mostly late in the game, but realistic
  placement comes first.

## 9. Battles

- Solitary and territorial Pokémon: always single.
- Most others: a small double chance (all grass allows doubles now).
- Highly social Pokémon (herds, flocks, schools, packs, colonies, those that call for help): a higher double chance and
  some triple.
- Partners: usually the same family (a herd of young and adults); "any Pokémon of the area" for some places, to make
  battles interesting.
- Never while fishing.

## 10. Outbreaks

- Tentative: a rare Pokémon of the area made common for a day (a hatching, a bloom, an outbreak of Tentacruel that
  clears the sea). Outbreaks can be limited by season and time. To be revisited.

## 11. Wanderers

- Each family's entry says how it behaves when seen in the grass: met as a wanderer, by steps or both; wanders at
  random, aggressively (predators, territorial ones) or passively (shy, prey); stands in the grass or floats over it
  (flyers, hoverers).
